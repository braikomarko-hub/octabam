The **dbx 160** compressor/limiter (1976), modelled from its schematic: the
true-RMS detector gives the 160's program-dependent attack and release, with
no attack or release knobs. KEY takes the detector from any track over a
light side-chain bus (one level word per track), so a kick can duck a bass
line on another track.

## Controls

| Page | Knob | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | THRS | threshold, −50 to 0 dBFS RMS |
| 1 | RATI | ratio: 1:1 at 0, 2:1 at 50, 4:1 at 75, 120:1 at 100; above 100 on to −1:1 |
| 1 | OUT | output gain, ±20 dB |
| 1 | KEY | OFF (this track) or T1–T8 (that track's level) |
| 1 | MIX | dry/wet; 0 = exact passthrough |
| 2 | KNEE | HARD (the 160) or OVER (a soft knee, like the 160A's OverEasy) |

It has an id of its own, so every stock effect stays in the chooser. It
cannot share an image with SIDECHAIN_COMPRESSOR (the same dispatcher hook).

## A first patch: the kick ducks the bass

1. Kick on T1, bass on T5.
2. On T5 choose dB666 in FX2 (or FX1).
3. KEY = T1, RATI about 75 (4:1), then lower THRS until the bass dips on
   every kick. OUT brings the level back.
4. For a softer pump, KNEE = OVER on page 2, or MIX below 127.

Without KEY (OFF) it is an ordinary 160 on its own track: THRS and RATI
only, the attack and release follow the music.
